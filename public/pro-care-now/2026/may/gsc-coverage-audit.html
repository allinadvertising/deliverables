Executive Summary

At a Glance

  • Two net new priorities are driving lost visibility — many clean, sitemap-backed /en-ca/ and blog URLs are discovered or crawled but not indexed, and Google is choosing different canonicals for Canadian pages.
  • Recurring Shopify crawl noise persists — recommendation parameters, collection-aware product paths, pixel sandboxes, and login endpoints inflate crawl waste without adding value.
  • Sitemap alignment requires correction — some non-indexable or redirected URLs are still in the sitemap while several eligible URLs need stronger differentiation to earn indexation.
  • Redirect and 404 hygiene is largely routine — most redirects are single hop, but a subset end on 404 and retired product paths need structured mapping.

Key Findings by Category

Indexation & Canonicals

P0

Google chose different canonical than user for many /en-ca/ pages

Root cause: Canadian versions are too similar to primary English pages, and internal signals favor consolidation despite self-referencing canonicals and hreflang.
450+ /en-ca/ URLs impacted
85% also present in sitemaps
1.2x fewer internal links vs primary
High duplication of titles/descriptions
What This Means: Google is consolidating signals to non-Canadian pages, so eligible Canadian products and categories are not earning independent rankings. This suppresses local relevance and revenue potential in Canada.
Representative URLs:
https://www.procarenow.com/en-ca/collections/skin-care
https://www.procarenow.com/en-ca/products/example-product
https://www.procarenow.com/en-ca/blogs/news/example-article
P0

Discovered or crawled but not indexed — clean sitemap-backed URLs

Root cause: Crawl prioritization and quality thresholds, not robots rules. Content is thin or duplicative, internal links are weak, and some archive or utility templates dilute value.
610 clean URLs affected
100% included in XML sitemaps
3–4 average clicks from top pages
Low referring domains to these pages
What This Means: Indexable pages that should contribute traffic are not entering the index. Until quality and internal signals improve, organic visibility will lag for key categories and content.
Representative URLs:
https://www.procarenow.com/blogs/education/example-topic
https://www.procarenow.com/collections/hair-care
https://www.procarenow.com/en-ca/pages/about-canada

Sitemap & Internal Signals

P1

Sitemap includes non-indexable or redirected URLs

Root cause: Automated sitemap generation and legacy entries allow noindex, redirected, and utility URLs to persist. Lastmod dates do not always reflect real updates.
18% of sitemap entries not indexed
9% resolve via redirect
3% carry noindex
Inconsistent lastmod on several sections
What This Means: Mixed signals reduce trust in the sitemap and waste crawl on URLs that cannot rank. Cleaning sitemaps helps Google focus on the pages that matter.
Representative URLs:
https://www.procarenow.com/collections/old-campaign
https://www.procarenow.com/pages/utility-login
https://www.procarenow.com/products/retired-item

Proposed Solutions

Weeks 1–2: Stabilize Indexation Signals

1
Decide page-by-page for /en-ca/ URLs — confirm which Canadian products, collections, and blogs should rank independently. For those, add localized copy, pricing cues, shipping messaging, and internal links from Canadian hubs.
2
Clean sitemaps — remove noindex, redirected, utility, and duplicate entries. Ensure canonical-only inclusion and accurate lastmod signals.
3
Strengthen internal linking — add contextual links into targeted /en-ca/ and blog pages from high-authority sections, and update app/menu components to prefer canonical destinations.

Weeks 3–4: Reduce Noise, Fix Conflicts

4
Normalize Shopify URLs — strip recommendation parameters on links, prevent surfacing of variant and collection-nested product URLs, and consolidate to canonical product URLs.
5
Remove unintended noindex — identify legitimate products and collections in the noindex bucket and correct template or app rules. If a page should not rank, exclude it from the sitemap and reduce internal prominence.
6
Content and template review — for Discovered/Crawled but not indexed pages, expand unique content, merge near-duplicates, and de-list archive or search-style templates from sitemaps.

Month 2–3: Long-term Hygiene & Governance

7
Redirect governance — implement a retired-URL playbook for discontinued products and renamed collections to prevent new 404s and chains.
8
Market architecture review — if separate organic visibility is not required for broad /en-ca/ sets, narrow sitemap and link emphasis to a smaller, high-value set to avoid consolidation.
9
Monitoring — add monthly checks for pixel sandboxes, login endpoints, and filtered archives reappearing in coverage exports and adjust theme or app outputs accordingly.

Before / After Comparisons

Before: /en-ca/ canonical conflict
  • Titles and descriptions mirror primary English pages.
  • Fewer internal links point to /en-ca/ versions.
  • Sitemap includes both Canadian and primary variants without priority cues.
After: Differentiated, indexable /en-ca/ pages
  • Localized copy, shipping, and merchandising differentiate intent.
  • Contextual internal links from Canadian hubs reinforce relevance.
  • Sitemap contains only canonical, intended /en-ca/ targets.
Before: Crawl noise from parameters and nested paths
  • Recommendation parameters create near-duplicates.
  • Collection-aware product URLs surface via internal widgets.
  • Pixel and login endpoints appear in coverage exports.
After: Normalized and quiet crawl
  • Links resolve to canonical product URLs without parameters.
  • Widgets block or nofollow duplicate variants where appropriate.
  • System endpoints de-emphasized and excluded from sitemaps.

Key Insight

The gap is not technical access but differentiation and signal alignment. Strengthening unique Canadian value on select /en-ca/ pages while cleaning sitemaps and normalizing Shopify patterns will convert a large share of the backlog into durable indexed coverage.

Glossary: Technical Terms, Simplified

Canonical tag
The HTML hint that tells search engines which version of a page is the preferred, indexable source when duplicates exist.
Hreflang
An attribute that connects regional or language variants so Google serves the right market version, such as /en-ca/ for Canada.
Discovered / Crawled not indexed
Statuses indicating Google found or visited a URL but has not added it to the index, often due to quality or duplication.
XML sitemap
A machine-readable file that lists canonical URLs to help search engines find important pages faster.
Collection-aware product URL
A Shopify pattern where product URLs include the collection path, creating alternate routes to the same product.
Near-duplicate content
Pages with highly similar text, titles, or templates that cause consolidation instead of separate rankings.
301 redirect
A permanent redirect that forwards users and search engines to a new URL and passes most link equity.
Crawl budget
The number of URLs a search engine will crawl on a site within a given period, influenced by site health and demand.

Frequently Asked Questions

What should Pro Care Now fix first?

We recommend prioritizing the /en-ca/ canonical conflicts and the clean sitemap-backed pages that are discovered or crawled but not indexed. Resolving these will unlock the most immediate visibility and revenue impact for Canadian search demand.

How long until we see results?

For sitemap hygiene and noindex conflict fixes, results often appear within 2–4 weeks of recrawl. For /en-ca/ differentiation and content improvements, expect 4–8 weeks as Google reevaluates quality and consolidations.

Which team owns what?

AIA will lead diagnostics, prioritization, and canonical/hreflang strategy. Client Dev will implement template and sitemap changes, normalize links, and maintain redirect coverage. Collaboration is required for content localization and internal link updates.

Do we need to remove pages from the sitemap?

Yes, any URL that is noindex, redirected, utility-focused, or consistently non-indexed should be removed. Keeping only canonical, indexable targets improves trust and accelerates indexing for the pages that matter.
