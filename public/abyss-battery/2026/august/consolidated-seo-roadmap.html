Executive summary

Abyss is in an impression-led organic decline. Over the last three months Google showed the site 301,980 fewer times, a 26.0 per cent drop, while clicks held better at 4.7 per cent down because brand searches, the homepage and key product pages still offset the losses.

The root cause is fragmented URL signals: encoded slugs, http and https variants, www and non-www hosts, tracking parameters and duplicate pages are all splitting authority that should point to one address. A third-party replatform lands around October 2026. If those signals are consolidated first, the new site inherits a clean footprint. If they are not, the replatform compounds the decline.

Six things to know

  • The decline is about visibility, not clicks yet. Impressions and average position fell hard. Clicks will follow unless the signal problems are fixed before the migration.
  • URL fragmentation is the main technical risk before October. A 45-row redirect map, a duplicate sitemap root and a dozen parameter patterns are ready to fix now.
  • The money cluster is 36V and 24V trolling motor batteries. Those two collections lost 610 clicks combined and carry the flagship B2C growth priority.
  • AI visibility is real and worth keeping. Nine of the top ten AI-visible pages are blog guides. They should be refreshed in place, never moved.
  • Merchant Center has a fast win. Twenty-two sale-price suggestions are worth about 93 clicks a week, pending your pricing approval.
  • Dated phasing matters as much as the task list. The plan totals 122 estimated hours, so every item below has a window, an owner and a status.

Operating principle

Consolidate before you create. Fix URL signals, blog overlap and measurement first, then build growth layers. That order is what lets the October replatform inherit cleaner signals instead of copying today's mistakes.

Crawl and index health

REF 01P0

Apply the 45-row redirect map

What we found

Encoded trademark symbols, http and https variants, www and non-www hosts, parameter URLs and off-taxonomy paths split ranking signals. The URL audit produced a 45-row deterministic 301 map, including 29 encoded product, page and blog slugs.

What we will do

Apply the 45 redirects in Shopify or at the replatform edge, then verify every source redirects once to a live https://www page. Ambiguous pairs stay out until direction is verified.

What you get

Duplicate URL signals consolidate before the new platform inherits the current search footprint.

Owner: AIA SEO + AIA Dev or replatform teamEffort: 6 hrsWindow: Days 0 to 7Status: Open. Do first. Blocks refs 03, 08 and 19.
REF 02P0

Consolidate the sitemap root

What we found

Both the http and https sitemap roots are submitted in Search Console, and www and non-www homepage variants both carry search data. Forty CDN asset URLs still resolve over http.

What we will do

Submit only the https://www sitemap root, remove the http submission and confirm every child sitemap serves over https. Historical variants are carried through redirects, not dropped.

What you get

Google receives one canonical sitemap root and one preferred host before the replatform, which also makes pre- and post-launch comparisons clean.

Owner: AIA SEOEffort: 2 hrsWindow: Days 0 to 7Status: Open. Do alongside ref 01.
REF 03P1

Verify duplicate-intent canonicals

What we found

Pairs such as privacy policy page versus policy endpoint, return versus refund policy, and word-order or brand-prefix variants of the 12V 120Ah, 48V 105Ah, 24V 100Ah and 36V 60Ah products compete for the same queries with no verified winner.

What we will do

Crawl and inspect each pair, choose the canonical target from GSC data, canonicals, internal links and live content, then append only verified 301s to the map.

What you get

Each product and policy intent resolves to one page before migration, so the new site does not carry two pages for one product.

Owner: AIA SEO + AIA DevEffort: 4 hrsWindow: Days 7 to 30Status: Verify first. Waits on ref 01.
REF 04P0

Remove tracking parameter URLs

What we found

Variant, country, currency, UTM, review-app and recommendation parameters generate non-canonical URLs that carry search data and consume crawl budget. Collections paginate to page 9 and the blog to page 32.

What we will do

Strip, canonicalise or noindex tracking and recommendation parameters while keeping real products, collections and blogs crawlable. No pattern is blocked until samples confirm it is not the only reachable product state.

What you get

Google spends less time on generated variants and more on canonical product, collection and blog URLs, and the 1,540 not-indexed count starts to reflect the real site.

Owner: AIA Dev (Shopify) + AIA SEOEffort: 6 hrsWindow: Days 7 to 30Status: Open. After refs 01 and 02.
REF 05P0

Audit noindex and robots rules

What we found

Search Console shows 240 pages excluded by noindex, 103 blocked by robots.txt and 5 indexed despite being blocked. With only 334 pages indexed, broad rules may be hiding useful content along with junk.

What we will do

Export both URL lists, classify each as legitimate content, duplicate, parameter, tag, pagination or utility, and lift blocks only where true content is affected.

What you get

Useful pages become indexable while low-value variants stay controlled, and the new site does not copy stale rules.

Owner: AIA SEO + AIA DevEffort: 5 hrsWindow: Days 7 to 30Status: Verify first. After refs 01, 02 and 04.
REF 06P1

Review redirect and 404 buckets

What we found

Search Console reports 267 redirecting pages, 251 canonical alternates and 45 not-found URLs. Ahrefs corroborates with 278 redirects, 76 404s and 324 other 4XX responses across 1,276 crawled pages.

What we will do

Classify each URL by value and source, remove internal links to dead or redirected addresses, and add specific 301s only where a live equivalent exists. No bulk redirect to the homepage.

What you get

The replatform starts with cleaner redirects, fewer dead URLs and no internal links pointing at non-final addresses.

Owner: AIA SEO + AIA Dev or replatform teamEffort: 7 hrsWindow: Days 7 to 30Status: Open. Informed by refs 01, 03 and 07.

How to read this roadmap

This document is the consolidation, sequencing and handoff view. The Abyss Battery Deep SEO Analysis remains the evidence record, and every figure here is carried from the August 2026 source audits captured on 27 and 28 August. No independent live verification was performed while building the roadmap.

ConfirmedThe source audit showed it directly. Every count and percentage in this document is confirmed unless noted.
InferredThe source supported the pattern but did not prove causation. Applies to a few plain redirect targets and the blog query-to-URL linkage.
OpenThe needed check or export was not available. Listed below so nothing is presented as settled when it is not.

What is still open

  • Renamed-SKU handle pairs. Confirmed as a problem, but the exact old and new handles are not in any source file.
  • Merchant Center product and feed status. Overview metrics are confirmed. The product list, feed status and store-quality screens were not visible.
  • Full Search Console URL exports per reason bucket. Bucket counts are confirmed. URL-level lists are exported inside refs 05 and 06.
  • Canonical direction for ambiguous duplicate pairs. Settled by a crawl in ref 03, never by naming preference.
  • Ahrefs organic estimates. Treated as directional because Ahrefs displayed a ranking-data disruption banner. Search Console is the source of truth for organic performance.
  • Blog publish dates. Not in the exports. A CMS review precedes any merge or deletion.

What was deliberately left out

  • Homepage and brand query growth is protected through redirects and monitoring rather than opened as a growth task, because it is the offset keeping clicks stable.
  • The fishing-locations lifestyle post, despite 4,921 AI impressions, has weak purchase intent and is evaluated inside blog consolidation only.
  • Broad Merchant Center feed fixes wait until the feed status screens are visible and diagnosed in ref 10.
  • Trailing-slash normalisation was found only at root level and is covered by refs 01 and 02.
